About

I am a PhD student in Astronomy at the University of Cambridge, based at the Institute of Astronomy and the Kavli Institute for Cosmology. My research sits at the intersection of cosmological inference and scientific computing: how the hot gas in galaxy clusters imprints on the cosmic microwave background, and how modern inference and agentic AI methods can be used to analyse that signal — and, more broadly, to carry out research.

A first strand of my work uses simulation-based inference to test the likelihood assumptions that underpin analyses of the thermal Sunyaev–Zel’dovich (tSZ) power spectrum. A second strand develops multi-agent systems, including cmbagent and Denario, that plan, code, and critique scientific workflows in cosmology.

Research

CMB secondaries

Thermal Sunyaev–Zel’dovich power spectra, cluster astrophysics, and the use of Compton-y maps as a cosmological probe.

Simulation-based inference

Neural posterior and likelihood estimation for non-Gaussian observables, and checks of when a Gaussian power-spectrum likelihood remains adequate.

AI for discovery

Planning-and-control multi-agent systems (cmbagent, Denario) and evolutionary coding agents (CMBEvolve, CosmoEvolve) for autonomous and semi-autonomous research in cosmology.
